# Water Availability, Rainfall and Soil Moisture

## Key relationship

Water availability in agricultural ecosystems depends on more than annual rainfall.

Relevant factors include:
- precipitation
- evapotranspiration
- soil moisture
- irrigation
- runoff
- soil and landscape characteristics

## Drought

Agricultural drought is strongly related to soil-moisture availability.

The IPCC distinguishes meteorological drought from agricultural drought because precipitation changes alone do not fully describe agricultural water stress.

Soil and hydro-morphological characteristics can modify drought impacts.

## Management implication

When rainfall is low or water availability is uncertain, recommendations should consider:
- soil moisture conservation
- water availability
- crop water requirements
- local soil characteristics
- seasonal rainfall patterns

Recommendations should not assume that a single annual rainfall number automatically determines biodiversity or crop outcomes.

## Relevance to Darukaa.Earth

Rainfall should be analyzed together with soil condition and land use.

For example:

Low rainfall
+
poor soil condition
+
low habitat diversity

may create interacting pressures that should be considered together.

## Evidence

Source: IPCC — Climate Change 2022: Impacts, Adaptation and Vulnerability, Chapter 4: Water
Organization: Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change

Key evidence:
Soil moisture is influenced by precipitation, evapotranspiration and irrigation. Soil and hydro-morphological characteristics also influence agricultural, ecological and hydrological drought.

Source: IPCC — Climate Change 2021: The Physical Science Basis, Chapter 10
Organization: Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change

Key evidence:
Climate change affects soil moisture and drought characteristics, with substantial regional differences and uncertainties.
