# Agroforestry and Ecosystem Resilience

## Definition

Agroforestry integrates trees with crops and/or livestock within agricultural systems.

It can provide multiple ecological and production functions within the same landscape.

## Potential benefits

FAO identifies potential agroforestry benefits including:
- improved soil health
- improved water management
- farm biodiversity
- reduced soil and wind erosion
- improved microclimates
- restoration of degraded land
- greater resilience to environmental shocks

## Multi-metric relationships

Agroforestry can connect several environmental variables:

Trees
? influence soil processes and organic matter inputs

Trees and vegetation
? influence water regulation and microclimate

Greater structural and species diversity
? can provide habitat and ecological connectivity

These relationships make agroforestry relevant to multi-metric environmental reasoning.

## Important limitation

Agroforestry outcomes depend on:
- tree and crop species
- climate
- soil
- management
- landscape context
- water availability

Therefore the system should not claim a universal percentage improvement from agroforestry without evidence for the specific context.

## Relevance to Darukaa.Earth

When soil degradation, water stress and low habitat diversity occur together, agroforestry may be considered as one potential integrated intervention.

The recommendation engine should retrieve evidence supporting the relevant mechanism rather than relying on a fixed percentage improvement.
