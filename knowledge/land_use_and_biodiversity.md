Land-use practices influence habitats, agricultural biodiversity and ecosystem functions.

## Agricultural Biodiversity

Agricultural biodiversity includes the diversity of crops, livestock, organisms and associated ecosystems that contribute to food and agricultural systems.

Maintaining diversity can support ecosystem functions such as pollination, pest regulation, nutrient cycling and habitat provision.

## Monoculture

Monoculture refers to agricultural systems where a single crop is cultivated over a large area or during a production period.

Monoculture can reduce crop and habitat diversity compared with more diversified systems.

The environmental effects of a land-use system depend on local ecological, climatic and management conditions.

## Crop Diversification

Context-appropriate diversification can include practices such as intercropping, crop rotation and integration of additional vegetation or habitat.

Diversification can provide different habitats and resources for organisms and can contribute to ecosystem resilience.

## Habitat Diversity

Habitat diversity provides different environmental conditions and resources for species.

Maintaining vegetation diversity and suitable habitats can support biodiversity and ecosystem functions.

## Biodiversity and Ecosystem Functions

Biodiversity can contribute to ecosystem functions including:

- Pollination
- Pest regulation
- Nutrient cycling
- Soil biological activity
- Habitat provision
- Ecosystem resilience

Environmental recommendations should consider interactions between land use, soil, water and biodiversity.

## Biodiversity in agricultural systems

Biodiversity for food and agriculture includes plants, animals and microorganisms at genetic, species and ecosystem levels.

It supports:
- healthy soils
- pollination
- pest regulation
- habitat
- ecosystem services
- resilience of production systems

## Land-use change

Important pressures on biodiversity include:
- agricultural intensification and expansion
- land-use change
- habitat alteration and loss
- climate change
- pollution
- changes to water cycles

These pressures can interact and amplify their effects.

## Diversification

FAO reports that diversification of production systems can improve resilience, support livelihoods and contribute to food security.

Examples include:
- using multiple crop species
- integrating crop, livestock and forest biodiversity
- increasing habitat diversity
- maintaining biodiversity within agricultural landscapes

## Relevance to Darukaa.Earth

Land-use recommendations should therefore consider both agricultural production and biodiversity.

A monoculture system should not automatically be classified as "bad"; the system should examine its characteristics and identify opportunities to increase useful diversity where appropriate.

Potential metrics include:
- crop diversity
- habitat diversity
- species richness
- pollinator habitat
- soil biodiversity
- landscape connectivity
